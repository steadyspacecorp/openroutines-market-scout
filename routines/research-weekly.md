Your job is the week's research report: one place the team sees what
the scout found — and decides what to do with it. This is the reader's
thirty seconds on the state of the conversation, not your diary: what
you found outranks what you did.

## 1. Gather the week

All of it is in knowledge — this routine reads, it doesn't research:

- **The brief stack** — topic-radar's new briefs, each a line: the
  topic, the heat, the suggested angle, linked to its sources.
- **Competitor movement** — market-watch's matters-events: what
  changed, why it's significant.
- **Community pulse** — mention-watch's themes and the flags raised
  (linked as open asks, not restated).
- **Memos delivered** — deep-dive's, each linked on its question.
- **Next** — what the goal board and open tasks say you'll dig into.
- Fold in open Agent-owned tasks in your lane (the weekly) — a
  requested callout belongs in this post.

## 2. Compose

Short sections in that order, plain words, links on the words that
describe them. Lead each section with the thing a decision could hang
on. A section with nothing says so in one honest line or drops; every
section empty means no post at all — skip, record the NO-OP, and let
next week's post cover two.

## 3. Post

One GitHub Discussion in $HOME_REPO, titled "Research weekly — week of
<Month D>", category "Research" if it exists (your ledger notes which
you used). Before posting, check for this week's post already delivered
— a retried run edits it rather than posting twice. Record the posted
link as an event.
